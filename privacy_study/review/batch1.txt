Staff Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI): GBEE-R2 Source: web page The purpose of this regulation is to protect staff, student, parent and 27J information and to direct staff on information that cannot be submitted to artificial intelligence applications/programs. For the purpose of this policy AI applications/programs includes Chat GPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, Apple AI, and other similar programs or applications. 27J employees and contractors shall not submit, store, or process in AI systems or tools the following: (1) personally identifiable information (PII); (2) protected health information (PHI); (3) District intellectual property; (4) District and school safety plans, information and protocols. District employees' use of AI shall comply with all applicable 27J internet and technology use policies, and comply with federal, state and local student privacy laws, including FERPA and the Colorado Student Data and Transparency Act.

D087-B136 | excerpt PDF page 22 | Source: pages 168–169 | Federal and Illinois Privacy, Confidentiality, and Records Laws
Within Illinois and beginning in the late 1950s, Illinois’ State Records Act (1957, 5 ILCS 160) and
Local Records Act (1961, 50 ILCS 205) established baseline expectations for how public entities
create, keep, and dispose of records. For school districts, as work moved from paper to email to
cloud platforms—and now to AI-assisted drafting and chat logs—these statutes remain the
backdrop for retention schedules, appropriate disposal, and defensible recordkeeping.

D087-B137 | excerpt PDF page 22 | Source: pages 168–169 | Federal and Illinois Privacy, Confidentiality, and Records Laws
Early federal privacy statutes introduced principles that still guide district practice. The Fair Credit
Reporting Act (FCRA, 1970) emphasized data accuracy and an individual’s right to correct
information used in decisions affecting them. The Privacy Act of 1974 established fair information
practice expectations for government-held data. In education, the Family Educational Rights and
Privacy Act (FERPA, 1974) anchored student education record protections—access, amendment,
and limits on disclosure. These early laws matter for AI as AI systems create new derived
information and can be mistakenly treated as “verdicts” rather than supports for decision-making. So
too, as AI uses the information available for its outputs, accuracy of data is essential to avoid
inaccuracies and hallucinations.

• Algorithmic surveillance reduces perceived autonomy and can impair performance: Participants

  (Balash et al. 2021)

  individuals and can expand to broad data scope; without safeguards, students can be treated as
  objects of prediction rather than participants with voice and choice. (Rubel & Jones 2016);
  (Prinsloo & Slade 2015)
• Minimize surveillance and provide opt-outs or alternatives where feasible: Learning analytics
  ethics emphasizes autonomy interests; opt-out structures and meaningful choice reduce student
  vulnerability and protect agency. (Rubel & Jones 2016); (Prinsloo & Slade 2015)

D171-B013 | excerpt PDF page 3 | Source: pages 11–12 | Prohibited Use of AI Tools: Privacy
• Privacy concerns: The use of AI is required to adhere to state and Federal privacy laws.
• Compromising Privacy: The education system will not use AI in ways that compromise teacher
  or student privacy or lead to unauthorized data collection, as this violates privacy laws and our
  system’s ethical principles. See the Security, Privacy, and Safety section below for more
  information.
• Noncompliance with Existing Policies: We will evaluate AI tools for compliance with all
  relevant policies and regulations, such as privacy laws and ethical principles. AI tools will be
  required to detail if/how personal information is used to ensure that personal data remains
  confidential and isn’t misused.

D171-B014 | excerpt PDF page 3 | Source: page 13 | Special Consideration: Security, Privacy, and Safety
We will implement reasonable security measures to secure AI technologies against unauthorized
access and misuse. All AI systems deployed within the school will be evaluated for compliance with
relevant laws and regulations, including those related to data protection, privacy, and students’
online safety. For example, providers will make it clear when a user is interacting with an AI versus a
human. Staff and students are prohibited from entering confidential or personally identifiable
information into unauthorized AI tools, such as those without approved data privacy agreements.
This extends to contracted vendors and other third parties. Sharing confidential or personal data
with an AI system could violate privacy if not properly disclosed and consented to.

D171-B015 | excerpt PDF page 3 | Source: pages 15–16 | Glossary (excerpt)
• Data Disposition: The process of disposing of data when it is no longer needed, ensuring it is
  securely destroyed or transferred according to policy.
• Data Privacy Agreement (DPA): An agreement between a local education agency (LEA) and a
  vendor which establishes data privacy and security protections for student data. Data privacy
  agreements are +-the primary method used for meeting Utah’s law requiring documentation of
  certain data protections in a contract.
• Responsible Use of AI: The ethical and compliant use of AI tools to ensure data transparency,
  security, privacy, and more.
• Walled Garden: A closed ecosystem where the provider has total control over applications,
  content, and media, restricting access to data.
