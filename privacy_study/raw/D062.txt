1. Ask for Permission Before Using AI Tools (excerpt)
Source: page 1

Do students need to ask parents/caregivers for permission to use AI tools?

• Students need to ask their parents/caregivers for permission before using AI tools on personal
  devices. Most AI tools have age restrictions. Review the privacy policy and terms of use. Find out
  the age restrictions, and what data is collected, used, and shared. Check whether you retain
  rights to data, the ability to delete data, and the ability to opt out of data sharing.
• For FCPS-Approved AI tools: Parent/caregiver permission may be needed to use
  FCPS-approved AI tools. Students and parents/caregivers can search the FCPS Digital
  Ecosystem Library (DEL) with "AI" (in quotes) to find FCPS-approved tools, and for whom it is
  approved. Teachers should check the status of tools before inviting students to use them.

3. Protect Data Privacy
Source: page 1

Be careful with the information you give to an AI tool. Some AI tools may keep the data you enter.

• Keep it General. Don’t put personal information such as your address, student ID number,
  grades, pictures, or recordings of yourself or others.
• Respect others' privacy and work. Don’t put someone else's work into an AI tool
• Think about the information that you share. Protect your creative work. Don’t put information into
  AI that you wouldn’t want someone else to know.
• Respect copyright. Don’t put original works that belong to FCPS or someone else. Some
  examples are published books and FCPS curriculum materials.
• Remember to ask for help. Parents, caregivers, and teachers can help understand how to use AI
  best. Reach out to others with questions.

4. Prioritize Learning: Avoid Unacceptable Uses of AI (excerpt)
Source: page 2 [graphic]

• [graphic] Do not enter personal information, or someone else’s work. Follow the guidance to
  Protect Data Privacy.
• [graphic] Do not use AI in ways that cause harm. Follow the Student Rights and Responsibilities
  and Acceptable Use Policy. Example: “Students should use technology ethically and responsibly.
  Students should not use technology to bully, intentionally mislead, harm, or violate the privacy of
  self or others.”
