Excerpted verbatim from North Dakota K-12 AI Guidance Framework (North Dakota Department of Public Instruction, web

Introduction (excerpt)
Source: web page, introduction

AI cannot exist on its own. It requires human input (in the form of data, questions or prompts, and
preprogrammed algorithms or mathematical instructions). This technology can process large
volumes of data with incredible speed, efficiency, and precision to produce an output.

Glossary: Large Language Model (excerpt)
Source: web page, glossary

Sometimes, companies include data sets of text without the creator’s consent.

Implementation Roadmap: Evaluate Technology Infrastructure
Source: web page, roadmap

Review current EdTech providers supplying AI to vet their safety, privacy, reliability, and efficacy to
determine if they are appropriate for your school and which users they will support based on their
terms of service

Age-Appropriate Use: Middle School (excerpt)
Source: web page, developmental stages

You should also note any age-level restrictions on some AI products. Some tools are prohibited for
children under age 13 or require parental/guardian permission.

Special Education: Data Collection and Analysis (excerpt)
Source: web page, special education

AI can also assist in data collection and analysis, helping educators understand student
performance and devise effective teaching strategies. AI-powered predictive analytics can help
educators to identify potential learning obstacles and provide timely intervention. By analyzing data
on student performance and behavior, AI can predict which students might be at risk of falling
behind and suggest strategies to help them catch up.
