I. Overview: Principle 4, Privacy and Data Security
Source: PDF pages 1–2

4. Privacy and Data Security Student and staff data must be protected. No personally identifiable
information … including Individual Education Plans (IEP), 504 Plans, … contents, student
performance data, or health information — should be entered into any AI tool. Exceptions may be
granted for specific tools that the District AI Committee and Technology Department have expressly
approved for Personally Identifiable Information use, provided a signed Student Data Privacy
Agreement is in place before any such data is entered.

Footnote 1: definition of personally identifiable information
Source: PDF pages 1–2

The term “personally identifiable information” includes, but is not limited to, name, address, phone
number, social security number, disability status, driver’s license number, medical history, student
… identification numbers, or any other information that can be used directly or indirectly, to identify,
contact or locate a specific student or individual.

II. Procedures (excerpt)
Source: PDF page 2

• Require that all new AI tools are brought to the District AI Committee for vetting prior to
  classroom use.

II. Community Engagement (excerpt)
Source: PDF page 3

Families should be aware that students may have access to AI tools at home that are not district
approved. The district cannot control AI use on personal devices or home networks; however,
academic integrity expectations apply to all student work regardless of where it is completed. This
includes only using district approved tools for schoolwork regardless of where or how it is
completed.
