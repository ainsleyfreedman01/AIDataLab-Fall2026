Your child’s safety is our top priority. Both Gemini and NotebookLM are classified as Google Core

General Questions & District Vision: Approved Tools (excerpt)
Source: web page, section 'General Questions & District Vision'

Which AI tools, if any, have been approved for use by the district?
Google's Gemini and NotebookLM are approved as Core Services within our Google Workspace for
Education accounts for all staff and for students in grades 6-12. Generative AI elements within other
district-approved curriculum tools such as Adobe, Canvas, and Google Classroom are also
approved as part of the comprehensive evaluation each tool undergoes before approval. Other tools
must be evaluated and approved by the district before use.

Data Privacy & Security
Source: web page, section 'Data Privacy & Security'

How is our district protecting student data when AI tools are used?
We only approve AI tools that meet our standards for data privacy. Our approved tools (Gemini,
NotebookLM) are “Core Services” in our Google Workspace, meaning they follow the same strict
privacy rules as Google Drive and Gmail.

Do the AI tools used by the district comply with federal and state privacy laws like FERPA
(Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act) and COPPA (Children's Online Privacy
Protection Act)?
Yes. Our district policy (EFEAA) requires all AI tools to comply with data privacy laws. The Google
Workspace for Education Core Services we use are governed by a robust privacy policy that affirms
their compliance with laws like FERPA and COPPA.

What student information (Personally Identifiable Information or PII) is being collected by
these AI tools, and why?

Google's Core Services for Education, which include Gemini and NotebookLM, do not collect PII for
commercial purposes. Like other Google Workspace apps, they process data to provide the service
(e.g., to process a prompt and return an answer) but this data is not used for advertising or other
commercial intentions.

Who owns the data that students input into AI platforms?
For our district's approved Google Core Services, Google's policy states that “Schools, not Google,
own their student and faculty data.”

Is student data being used to train AI models?
No. For our approved Google Workspace Core Services (Gemini and NotebookLM), student chats,
prompts, and uploaded files are never used to train AI models and are never shared outside the
district.

What security measures are in place to prevent data breaches related to AI tools?
Our approved AI tools are part of the Google Workspace for Education, which is built with strong,
multi-layered security features, including data encryption and multi-factor authentication, to protect
data from threats.

Support for Teachers & Staff (excerpt)
Source: web page, section 'Support for Teachers & Staff'

What guidelines are teachers following for using AI to communicate with parents or assess
student work?
Staff must use district-approved tools, supervise student use, and never upload confidential or
sensitive student information (like grades or PII) into an AI tool. They must also carefully evaluate
any AI-generated content for accuracy and appropriateness before using it.

Broader Impacts & Future Planning: Reviewing and Approving New
AI Tools (excerpt)
Source: web page, section 'Broader Impacts & Future Planning'

How does the district review and approve new AI tools for classroom use?
Approved tools are determined by district personnel after a review of their security, privacy, data
usage, and academic integrity standards. Staff are not permitted to use or have students sign up for
unapproved AI platforms.
