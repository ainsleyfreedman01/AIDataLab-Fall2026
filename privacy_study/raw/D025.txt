Frequently Asked Questions: Privacy, Data, and Monitoring
(excerpts)
Source: web page, FAQ section, questions 9, 18-23, 29, 32, 43, 48-50, 54, 60-63, 71, 74

9. How are teachers trained?
… Specific to Artificial Intelligence, current and planned training includes responsible use, ethics,
privacy, instructional best practices, human oversight, and guidance on when Artificial Intelligence
should and should not be used.

18. How are Artificial Intelligence tools selected and approved?
District-approved tools are reviewed for instructional value, privacy, safety, accessibility, age
appropriateness, teacher training needs, responsible use, and alignment with district goals.

19. What are real-time threat detection and real-time moderation?
Real-time threat detection means a tool can alert adults quickly if a student interaction includes
serious concerns such as self-harm or threats to others. Real-time moderation means the tool

PDF EXCERPT PAGE 3
redirects or stops conversations that move into unsafe, inappropriate, or overly personal areas.

20. Who receives student safety alerts?
For approved student-facing tools, teachers receive alerts when a serious threat is detected.
Depending on the level of concern, the alert may also be escalated to administrators and other
trained personnel.

21. How is student information protected?
The district reviews approved tools for privacy and security requirements. Relevant protections
include the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), the Children’s Online Privacy
Protection Act (COPPA), and the Protection of Pupil Rights Amendment (PPRA). Florida protects
K-12 student data through the Student Online Personal Information Protection Act (Florida
SOPIPA), State Board of Education Rule 6A-1.09550, and the Florida Information Protection Act
(FIPA). Student information in approved tools stays within the Broward County Public Schools
environment and is not used to train Artificial Intelligence.

22. Do student interactions train the Artificial Intelligence models?
No. Under current district requirements, approved Broward County Public Schools tools must not
use our students’ interactions to train Artificial Intelligence models.

23. Are ChatGPT, Google AI, or other open tools used?
Open public tools such as ChatGPT and Google Artificial Intelligence features are not approved by
Broward County Public Schools as student-facing instructional tools. They may still be accessible
outside district-controlled settings. The district is reviewing controls for open public tools on
school-issued devices, including devices used on home networks. Access may be more difficult to
control when students use devices on home networks. Families are encouraged to remind students
to use only teacher-directed, district-approved tools for schoolwork and to discuss safe, responsible
technology use at home.

29. Will students be taught about the risks and limits of Artificial Intelligence?
… This includes checking accuracy, avoiding overreliance, protecting privacy, using tools ethically,
and verifying generated information.

32. What would cause the district to stop using a tool?
… If evidence and data show that a tool does not support district goals, improve learning, protect
students, or meet safety and privacy requirements, the district reconsiders, adjusts, replaces,
suspends, or discontinues its use.

43. Are teachers being trained to use Artificial Intelligence responsibly and effectively?
Yes. The district is providing professional learning focused on responsible use, privacy, safety,
instructional quality, and human oversight. …

48. Do students use their own district login when accessing approved Artificial Intelligence
tools?
Approved tools are made available through the student access portal, known as Clever. Students
can access only the tools available for their grade level and instructional setting. Some features

must also be assigned or enabled by a teacher.

49. Is a student’s work in MagicStudent private?
MagicStudent is not currently available to Broward County Public Schools students. Information
entered into approved tools stays within the district environment and is not used to train Artificial
Intelligence models. Approved tools allow authorized teachers or administrators to review student
activity for instruction, safety, and appropriate use.

50. Are students’ Individualized Education Programs (IEP) entered into MagicSchool?
At this time, details from a student’s Individualized Education Program are not automatically loaded
into MagicSchool. The district currently has this feature turned off. The district team is reviewing
features and processes to ensure that, if activated, the feature will align with district and state
standards and follow Broward County Public Schools privacy requirements.

54. How will the district respond if state law requires parent permission before students use
Artificial Intelligence?
The district will follow any final legal and policy requirements. The Broward Powered by AI site is
intended to provide parent notices, available choices, contacts, and updates. Because requirements
may change, families will receive clear information before any student-facing Artificial Intelligence
tool is introduced. …

60. Do approved Artificial Intelligence tools allow adults to review what students enter, and
who can see it?
Approved student-facing tools include monitoring, moderation, and flagging features. Teachers
oversee student use, and safety concerns are shared with appropriate school administrators. The
exact level of visibility depends on the tool and the educational or safety purpose. Student
information is protected, remains within the district environment, and is shared only with authorized
staff who need it.

61. Can parents review their child’s prompts and work in approved Artificial Intelligence
tools?
As with other student work, parents may ask the teacher or school to explain how a tool is being
used and may ask questions about their child’s work.

62. Will parents need to give permission before schools use Artificial Intelligence with their
child?
The district will follow final state law, School Board policy, and district procedures. Current family
resources are designed to provide notice, available choices, contacts, and updates. Because
requirements may change, families will receive clear information before a student-facing tool is
introduced. …

63. Who trains the Artificial Intelligence systems used by Broward County Public Schools?
Artificial Intelligence models are developed and trained by vendors using large data sets before the
district approves them. Broward County Public Schools reviews tools for privacy, safety,
accessibility, reliability, and instructional value. District-approved tools do not use Broward County
Public Schools students or their data to train Artificial Intelligence models.

Myths vs. Facts (excerpt)
Source: web page, FAQ section, 'Myths vs. facts'

Myth: Student data is used to train district-approved Artificial Intelligence tools.
Under current district requirements, student information in approved tools must remain within the
Broward County Public Schools environment and cannot be used to train Artificial Intelligence
models.

Myth: The district is relying only on vendors to decide whether tools are safe.
The district reviews student-facing tools for instructional value, privacy, safety, and other
requirements, and performs its own rigorous vetting process, including adversarial prompting,
real-time threat detection, and real-time moderation for student-facing tools.

Parent Resources: How Student Data Is Protected
Source: web page, section 'Parent Resources'

The protections behind every AI decision. Read the plain-language answer first, then open the full

Policy 7540.08, at its September 15, 2026 meeting.

is the guarantee that all student-facing generative AI tools need to provide real-time threat detection,

Sample answers for layout. Final wording to be confirmed by BCPS legal and data governance,
including any Florida-specific privacy law.

What information is collected, and why?
Approved tools collect only what is needed to deliver the educational service. Information is used for
that purpose alone, not for advertising or any unrelated commercial use.

Where is data stored, and who has access?
Data stays within the district's protected systems and approved vendor environments governed by
data privacy agreements. Access is limited by role to the staff who need it for instruction and
support.

Can AI train on my child's information?
No. Approved tools are configured so student and staff information is not used to train public AI
models. This is a condition of approval for every tool listed on this site.

What are my rights as a parent?
Parents have the right to know what AI tools are being used in their child's education and how they
are being used, and to request that their child be excluded from the use of AI where practicable.
Under FERPA you may also inspect and review your child's education records and request
corrections; under PPRA, certain surveys require notice and the chance to opt out.

How are tools checked before students use them?
All student-facing tools are subjected to a rigorous review process to ensure they include
protections beyond the already stringent BCPS data privacy and security provisions. Each tool must

demonstrate real-time threat detection and moderation, with immediate notification to teachers and
administrators of any inappropriate usage. Vendors also sign data privacy agreements and must
meet COPPA requirements for younger students; approved tools may not sell student data or use it
for targeted advertising.

The laws that protect your child
• FERPA Federal law giving parents rights over education records and control of personal
  information.
• COPPA Federal rule protecting the online data of children under 13. Vendors must comply.
• PPRA Requires notice and opt-out for certain surveys on sensitive topics.
• State privacy law Limits how education vendors use student data, including a ban on selling it.
