How the District Uses AI Safely and Responsibly
The district may use artificial intelligence tools to support learning, communication, and day-to-day
operations. When we do, protecting student and staff information is a top priority. Below is a clear
explanation of how we safeguard data and respect privacy.

Protecting Student and Staff Information
When AI tools are used, the district takes steps to protect students, staff, and district information
from misuse, leaks, or unauthorized access. Any use of AI must follow existing district technology
and privacy rules, as well as federal and state privacy laws, including laws that protect student
education records.

Using Only the Information That Is Necessary
We limit the amount of information shared with AI tools.

• Staff are encouraged to use summaries or examples instead of full student records.
• Whenever possible, information is removed, changed, or made anonymous before it is used.
• AI tools should be given only what is needed to complete a task, nothing more.
Keeping AI Tools Secure
AI tools are not connected to school systems like email, grading systems, or payroll unless the
district’s IT department has approved it. This helps reduce the risk of data breaches or account
compromise. All AI use must meet the district’s cybersecurity and technology use standards.

Protecting Personal Information

Personal information must always be handled carefully when using AI tools. Personal information
includes things like:

• Student names, ID numbers, or photos
• Grades, attendance, or discipline records
• Health or special education information
• Staff personnel records
This type of information is not entered into public or consumer AI tools unless the district has
approved it. Even small pieces of information can sometimes identify a person when combined with
other details, so care is taken to avoid that.

NOTE: Microsoft Copilot Chat is the only district-approved AI tool for use with personal, confidential,
or private information, and staff must not enter this type of information into any other AI tool.

Following Privacy Laws (FERPA)
All uses of AI follow the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) and other privacy laws.

• Student education records are only shared when legally allowed—such as with family permission
  or for a valid educational reason.
• If a vendor’s AI system uses student data, the vendor must follow strict rules and can only use
  that data for approved educational purposes under district control.
Being Transparent with Families
We believe trust comes from openness.

• The district will clearly explain when AI tools are used, why they are used, and how information is
  protected.

Overview of AI Tools in Use
Source: page 9

Bellevue School District uses a set of approved AI tools that have been reviewed for privacy,
security, educational or operational value, and alignment with district values, and that include
safeguards for student and staff data. Approved tools include Magic School, Colleague AI and
Microsoft Copilot. Microsoft Copilot Chat is the only district-approved AI tool for use with personal,
confidential, or private information, and staff must not enter this type of information into any other AI
tool.

Staff should not use personal or unapproved AI tools for school or district business unless explicitly
authorized.

Procurement and Vendor Requirements
Source: page 10

All AI tools must follow district procurement and vendor review processes.

Before approval, vendors must meet district privacy, security, and legal requirements, including
signing data privacy agreements and clearly explaining how district data is collected, stored,
deleted, and protected. Vendors may not use district data to train AI models unless explicitly
authorized by the district.

AI Tool Evaluation and Approval Process
• The tool is reviewed for data protection, legal compliance, security, educational or operational
  value, and equity and accessibility considerations.
Approval is required before an AI tool is used with students or district data.

Monitoring and Re-evaluating AI Tools
Approved AI tools are monitored over time for vendor or policy changes, reported concerns, and
continued alignment with district expectations. If concerns arise or a tool is no longer appropriate for
district use, it may be restricted, re-evaluated, paused, or removed, and the district will communicate
expectations to staff, address data retention or deletion, and update the approved tools list.
