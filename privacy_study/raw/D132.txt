Safety, Privacy, and Approval Pathways
Source: web page, section 'Safety'

AI work in OCPS is anchored in human review, student-data protection, monitoring, and a formal
review pathway for digital tools.

Every AI tool a student uses sits behind layers of district protection. Choose a view, then click
through each step to see how OCPS protects its network and protects students as they learn with
AI.

Protecting our network: five layers of network protection
Every tool is reviewed first — Before an AI tool reaches a classroom, district teams review its
contract, student-data privacy, cybersecurity, and educational value.

Only tools that meet district standards are allowed. Tools that don't pass are blocked or redirected.

District-managed accounts — Students and staff sign in with their OCPS accounts, so AI access
runs through district settings and safeguards instead of personal consumer accounts.

School AI use happens inside a protected educational workspace, not the open consumer version of
a tool.

Content filtering — District filtering screens web traffic on the district network, blocking
inappropriate content and AI tools that have not been approved.

Monitoring for safety — Activity on district resources may be monitored for compliance and safety,
helping staff notice and respond to concerns.

There is no expectation of privacy on district systems. That visibility is what lets adults step in when
something looks wrong.

Student data stays protected — Approved enterprise AI tools do not use OCPS data to train
public AI models, and sensitive information may only be entered into tools approved for that type of
data.

At the center of every layer is your student and their information.

Protecting students using AI: five steps of student protection
Age-appropriate AI experiences — Student tools, such as the Gemini youth experience, add
safeguards and age-aware responses designed for learners under 18.

Personal information stays private — Students learn not to share personal or confidential
information with AI, and approved tools are vetted for how they handle student data.

Privacy is protected by both the tool and the student's own habits.

Adults stay in the loop — Teachers review student work and AI outputs, and important decisions
about students are always made by people, never by AI.

Guardrails: Data Protection, Enterprise Tools, Monitoring
Source: web page, Safety section (guardrail cards)

Data Protection — Confidential data, sensitive student records, and PII may only be entered into
tools approved for that data classification.

Enterprise Tools — Staff use District-vetted, secure AI systems that do not use OCPS data to train
public models.

Monitoring — Users do not have an expectation of privacy on District resources. AI activity may be
monitored for compliance and safety.

Professional Learning — The District makes professional learning focused on AI literacy, data
privacy, and ethical integration available to staff.

AI safety and security — Tools are evaluated for contracts, data privacy, cybersecurity, system
compatibility, educational value, filtering, and monitoring needs.
