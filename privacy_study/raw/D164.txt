Data Privacy and Security Standards
Source: web page, Data Privacy and Security Standards

The use of all AI tools are treated in the same way as all other software applications. All staff and
students are required to use technology in support of the education experience which includes using
AI in a safe manner that protects all stakeholders.

This looks like never sharing personal details of students, staff, or other confidential information with
AI tools. This includes, but is not limited to: full names, addresses, phone numbers, ID numbers,
educational records, personnel records, health information, or financial information. When using
approved AI tools, it is still preferred to anonymize information to avoid the potential sharing of PII.

Only using approved AI tools is one of the many ways you can safeguard this information. Using
publicly available AI tools or personal accounts with AI tools can release this information and make
it accessible to those that should not have access.

Take these steps in your use of AI tools to ensure you are being safe and responsible.

• Only use approved AI tools.
• Never provide confidential, sensitive, or personally identifiable information when using AI tools.
• Never use copyrighted materials (such as District curriculum) with AI tools without express
  permission by the owner to do so.

Student Misuse Guidelines for Staff and Administrators
Privacy Violations: Any student or staff member who enters Personally Identifiable Information
(PII) into an unapproved AI tool will be subject to an administrative investigation and may face
disciplinary action for violating data privacy laws.

How Staff Might Misuse AI: Violating Data Privacy
Source: web page, Guidelines for Staff

• Enter personally identifiable information (PII) such as a student's name and educational record
  into a public AI tool that is not approved by the district.
• Share security related information with an unapproved AI tool such as a school's emergency
  management plan.

Consequences for Staff Misuse (excerpt)
Source: web page, Consequences for Staff Misuse

Staff members who violate AI use prohibitions or data privacy standards are subject to discipline
according to Board Policy GBCB (Staff Conduct) and Policy EHBC (Data Governance and Security)
or other board policies as applicable.

• Administrative Leave or Suspension: In cases where an investigation is needed—such as a
  suspected data breach or harassment—a staff member may be placed on administrative leave
  (with Human Resources approval) while the district reviews the situation.
• Termination of Employment: Serious violations, such as entering Personally Identifiable
  Information (PII) into unvetted tools, creating altered media to harass others, or using AI for
  significant academic/professional dishonesty, are grounds for dismissal.
Key Compliance Standards
• Privacy Violations: Any employee who improperly handles student or staff data will be subject
  to an administrative investigation and may face disciplinary action for violating state and federal
  data privacy laws.

Guidelines for Community: Protect Student Privacy and Safety
Source: web page, Guidelines for Community

Protecting the well-being of each student is our top priority, and that includes keeping their private
data safe.

• Keep PII Private: Never enter a student's Personally Identifiable Information (PII)—such as full
  names, ID numbers, home addresses, or grades—into public AI tools like ChatGPT.
• Use District-Approved Tools: Encourage your child to use only the AI applications that have
  been officially vetted and approved by the district to ensure they meet strict safety laws like
  FERPA.
• Monitor Age Rules: Be aware that many AI tools have strict age limits (often 13 or 18). Always
  follow the district's rules regarding parental consent for these technologies.

Implementation: AI Safety Course and Age-Appropriate Access
(excerpt)
Source: web page, Implementation

AI Safety Course
All staff members are required to complete a baseline safety course that covers how to protect
student privacy and avoid entering Personally Identifiable Information (PII) into AI tools.

Age-Appropriate Access
Students under 14 will only see AI through teacher-led demonstrations and embedded into
curriculum and assessment platforms. Students 14 and older may use approved tools
independently if they have a specific teacher's permission.

FAQ (excerpt)
Source: web page, Appendix, FAQ

Is my personal information safe when using AI?
Protecting your privacy is a top priority, and we only use tools that comply with strict laws like
FERPA. Users should never enter "Personally Identifiable Information," like full names or student ID
numbers, into public AI tools.

Which AI tools are allowed at SPS?
To ensure safety, stakeholders should only use AI applications that have been officially vetted and
approved by the district. We maintain a "Vetted Tools List" to help students and staff know which
tools meet our high standards for data privacy.

Are AI-enabled wearables, like smart glasses, allowed in class?
Currently, AI-enabled wearables such as smart glasses or pins are not permitted on campus.

Glossary (excerpt)
Source: web page, Appendix, Glossary

• Data Privacy: The practice of protecting personal information from being collected or used by AI
  companies without permission.
• Personally Identifiable Information (PII): Private details that can identify a specific person,
  such as full names, student ID numbers, home addresses, or grades.
