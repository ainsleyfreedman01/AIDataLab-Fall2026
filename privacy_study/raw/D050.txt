Introduction: Executive Summary (excerpt)
Source: page 3

While new technologies have contributed to innovations in education, they can create additional
risks, especially in the areas of student safety and privacy. Intentional policies and guidance can
help mitigate the risks of AI use.

Considerations for Secure Use: Procurement
Source: page 5 [graphic]

[graphic] When evaluating AI tools for procurement, OSSE recommends that LEAs ensure that
enterprise tools meet rigorous benchmarks prior to adoption.

[graphic] The following is a representative list of considerations prior to procurement:

[graphic] Training Protocols and Data Policy:
• [graphic] Protection of user generated data, including ensuring that vendors are not leveraging
  student data for model training, product improvement or purposes outside of the contracted
  service.

• [graphic] Protocols for data persistence and retrieval, including a technical understanding of how
  data is stored and deleted.
[graphic] Online Safety:
• [graphic] Inclusion of robust online safety features that are compliant with all District and federal
  laws.
[graphic] Cybersecurity:
• [graphic] AI systems that have verified compliance with recognized cybersecurity and privacy
  frameworks, such as the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), COPPA, and
  FERPA.
• [graphic] Systems featuring robust access controls, including multi-factor authentication, and
  encrypt sensitive data both in transit and at rest.
• [graphic] Vendors providing clear procedures for vulnerability and incident reporting, supporting
  secure configuration and regular updates, and demonstrating compliance with security and
  liability requirements – with special attention given to protecting personally identifiable
  information (PII) and ensuring that AI tools are only used in approved, secure environments.
• [graphic] The presence of ongoing monitoring, and documented incident response plans, and
  prohibition of unauthorized or consumer-grade AI tools.

Model Policy: Ethical Principles and Definitions (excerpt)
Source: page 7 [graphic]

• [graphic] Adopting emerging technologies requires an intentional approach to safeguard
  stakeholder safety and privacy: While technology can improve staff efficiency and
  effectiveness, all staff within [replace with LEA name] are ultimately accountable for student
  safety, development, and outcomes. Decision-making related to implementing AI will be made
  with staff, student, and community safety and privacy at the forefront, and with careful
  consideration safeguarding student and staff PII.
• [graphic] Enterprise tools: Software products for which the LEA has a specific license that
  provides features and protections not available in free versions.
• [graphic] Personally Identifiable Information (PII): PII is information that, alone or in
  combination with other data, can be linked to a specific employee including but not limited to:
• [graphic] Name of employee or other family members.
• [graphic] Address of employee or other family members.
• [graphic] Personal identifier, such as a Social Security Number, unique student or staff identifier
  (such as the Office of the State Superintendent of Education’s Unique Faculty and Staff
  Identifier), or biometric record; and indirect identifiers, such as date of birth, place of birth, or
  mother’s maiden name.

Summary of Staff AI Use Allowances (excerpt)
Source: page 9

STAFF AI USE NOT PERMITTED
AI cannot be used for final decision-making regarding staff and students, or in situations that require
special considerations for privacy or present risks to student well-being.

• Monitoring Students’ Physical Presence
LIMITED STAFF AI USE PERMITTED: PROCEED WITH ENHANCED OVERSIGHT
For any use case involving PII, only enterprise tools that comply with all local and federal laws and
regulations about student information can be used.

Red: Monitoring or Surveilling Staffs’ and Students’ Physical
Presence and Behavior
Source: page 10

The use of AI for the purpose of monitoring or surveilling staffs’ and students’ physical presence and
behavior includes tracking staffs’ and students’ physical presence within [replace with LEA name],
allowing AI to access CCTV footage captured by [replace with LEA name] security systems, and
use of biometric data for any monitoring purposes. This includes the use of personal AI-enabled
recording tools such as wearables while on [replace with LEA name] premises.

STAFF AI USE NOT PERMITTED
No AI tools may be used for this purpose. Unauthorized AI features of existing enterprise tools are
prohibited.

Rationale: Tools that monitor or surveil staff and students raise significant privacy concerns and AI
tools may cause discriminatory outcomes. There is insufficient evidence about if and how AI tools
can be used safely for this purpose.

Monitoring or Surveilling Staffs’ and Students’ Digital Presence
The use of AI for the purpose of monitoring or surveilling staffs’ and students’ digital presence
includes monitoring or surveilling online activity of staff and students while on [replace with LEA
name] premises or utilizing devices provided by [replace with LEA name].

LIMITED USE REQUIREMENTS:
• [Designated Staff Member] may utilize approved AI tools to assist with monitoring staffs’ and
  students’ digital activity while on LEA premises and during operating hours.
• AI surveillance is not a substitute for educator monitoring of student use of classroom devices or
  staff judgement about student wellbeing.
• Discipline decisions should never rely on data gathered by AI alone; all detected risks and
  misuse reports will be elevated to [Designated Staff Member] who will then follow [replace with
  LEA names] established discipline, safety, and mental health policies.
• Staff must keep data on AI surveillance use and continually monitor for equity and efficacy of
  use.
• Staff, students, and families will be notified about the tools and purposes for AI use in virtual
  surveillance, including which tools are being deployed, why they are being utilized, and what data
  is collected while monitoring a student’s virtual activity.

Rationale: Schools already monitor student online activity to remain in compliance with CIPA, and
acceptable use policies preclude the expectation of privacy on [replace with LEA name] issued

Drafting Lesson Plans
• Lesson plans should always be developed without utilizing prompts that invite an AI tool to
  consider student demographic information.
Rationale: Educators can use AI to improve lesson plans without sharing PII or relying on AI for final
decision-making.

Customizing Student-Facing Lesson Materials
• AI may support educators in developing differentiated and customized student-facing materials if
  it does not make recommendations for individual students or if it does not utilize PII and the
  educator reviews and refine the AI-generated suggestions.
