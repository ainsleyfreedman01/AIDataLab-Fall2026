Overall Expectations: Age and Parental Permission Note (excerpt)
Source: page 3

NOTE: Keep in mind that some GenAI tools, for example ChatGPT, require a user to sign up for an
account using an email address and phone number. ChatGPT is currently restricted to users aged
13 and up, with parent permission required for users aged 13-17.

The district could modify the paragraph below to prohibit all student use of GenAI regardless of
whether use is compliant with Education Law 2-d.

Staff must also be mindful that some GenAI tools are free and others are not, that some GenAI tools
are not permitted to be used by those under certain ages, or require parent permission, and that
some students and/or parents may not wish to use GenAI tools or input their work into them.

III. AI Tool Approval and Review
Source: page 3

NEW NOTE: This section is included to reflect best practices, to aid in compliance with data privacy
laws, to ensure consistency, and minimize redundancy of software.

All requests to use software/tools containing AI must be submitted to the Director of Technology [or
insert appropriate title], including the intended reasons for and uses of the software/tool. The
Director of Technology is responsible for reviewing and approving any software containing AI, in
consultation with the Data Protection Officer, to ensure they are compliant with the law and district
policies.

The Director of Technology will document the decisions that have been made to evaluate and
approve AI software/tools. The Director of Technology will periodically review the AI tools in use in
the district for continued compliance with the law and district policies.

IV. Acceptable and Prohibited Uses of Artificial Intelligence (excerpt)
Source: pages 4–5

NOTE: The district may wish to restrict the use of GenAI by employees for certain purposes. For
example, whether teachers should be allowed to use GenAI to grade student assignments, or within
what parameters, given that any student work input into a GenAI tool could be used by the platform,
and that GenAI can sometimes produce incorrect answers.

Any district approved, provided or designated AI tools are the only ones that may be used for
district, employment, or school-related purposes.

Users may not use the district’s approved AI tools, or use AI tools with district technology or
networks, to access, create, distribute, or display content that is harmful, deceptive, threatening,
obscene, disruptive, or sexually explicit, or that is harassment of others, in violation of district
policies, or interact with the AI in a manner that supports any of the above. This includes
“deepfakes” where an image, video, or audio is generated of an individual, particularly to make it
appear as though that person did or said something they did not.

The district also notes that non-consensual distribution of intimate images is a Class A
Misdemeanor under Penal Law §245.15.

V. Data Privacy and Restrictions on Entering Information into AI
Tools
Source: pages 5–6

NEW NOTE: Any use of student Personally Identifiable Information must be in conformance with
Education Law 2-d for third parties who have access to this information. Other categories of
information are protected by separate state laws, for example “private information” under State
Technology Law 208, or employee “personal identifying information” under Labor Law 203-d.

Any use of AI in schools, on school networks/computers, or for school purposes must be compliant
with state laws and regulations whenever sensitive information is entered into AI tools, including
under third-party agreements. Staff must consult with the district’s Data Protection Officer to
determine compliance with Education Law §2-d.

NOTE: In the paragraph below, the district may not wish to draw a distinction between public-facing
AI tools and "closed" systems, and prohibit the inputting of sensitive information into any AI tools. In
that case, simply delete the phrase "which can be used by the public" below.

Because any information used as input in AI tools may be used by that system and potentially made
available to other users of the system, district users must not input any personally identifying,
sensitive, copyrighted, proprietary, or confidential district information into an AI tool which can be
used by the public, consistent with applicable state and federal laws and district policies, including
but not limited to the following:

• Student information: anything that would be protected by FERPA (see policy 5500 and regulation
  5500-R) or Education Law 2-d (see policy 8635 and regulation 8365-R).
• Staff information: anything that would be protected by Education Law 2-d or Labor Law 203-d
  (see policy 8635 and regulation 8365-R), or other confidential personnel information.
• School district information: anything that would be withheld from a Freedom of Information Law
  (FOIL) request (see policy 1120 and regulation 1120-R), or private information under State
  Technology Law §208 which would require notification in the event of a breach.
Additionally, users of GenAI must exercise caution before inputting any work created by district
students or employees.
