Introduction (excerpt)
Source: page 3

By following these guidelines, administrators may help support safe, equitable, and effective AI use
while maintaining educator oversight, protecting student privacy, and fostering transparency with
stakeholders.

Essential Practices to Consider: Protect Privacy and Integrity
Source: page 3

• Protect Privacy and Integrity
Districts should be aware of and follow federal privacy and accessibility laws.

• Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) - Follow federal requirements for protecting
  students’ education records. Useful resources include the U.S. Department of Education’s
  FERPA overview and technical assistance tools.
• Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) - For online tools and applications used with
  children under 13, ensure parental consent and proper data collection practices, following the
  Federal Trade Commission's COPPA FAQs: COPPA compliance guide.

Phase 1: Compliance and Risk Controls
Source: page 6

Districts may consider:
• Aligning organizational practices to NIST AI Risk Management Framework (RMF).
• Having an AI team review compliance with:
• FERPA
• COPPA
• Children’s Internet Protection Act (CIPA)
• Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA)/K12 Security Information Exchange
  (SIX) cybersecurity baseline.

Phase 2: Procurement and Vendor Due Diligence
Source: page 8

Districts may consider updating a request for proposal template to include an expanded vendor
questionnaire covering AI-specific requirements. This may include:

• Model cards: model cards provide standardized documentation that describes an AI model’s
  intended use, training data sources, performance, limitations, and known risks. Model cards help
  the district verify whether a vendor’s AI system is appropriate, transparent, and safe for district
  use.
• Data sources and licensing: information on where AI model training data originated, whether it is
  lawfully obtained, and whether it includes proprietary or sensitive material.
• Privacy terms: how data is stored, protected, and used within the system.
• Data retention and deletion policies: how long the system retains data, and the processes for
  securely deleting it.
• Right to audit provisions: the district’s ability to review vendor practices, documentation, and
  controls to ensure compliance.
Districts may consider utilizing AI tool evaluations.
