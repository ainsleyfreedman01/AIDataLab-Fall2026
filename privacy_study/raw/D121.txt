How Teachers and School Staff Members May Use AI (excerpt)
Source: web page, section 'How Teachers and School Staff Members May Use AI'

Teachers and other school staff members may continue using AI for instructional planning and
operational tasks, subject to existing requirements, including the NYCPS Enterprise Request
Management Application (ERMA) data privacy and security approval process, and NYCPS
procurement rules. Additionally, AI may not be used for grading, behavior monitoring, or for
placement, promotion, graduation, and other decisions about students.

How Software is Evaluated Before Reaching Your Child's School
Source: web page, section 'How Software is Evaluated Before Reaching Your Child's School'

Before any tool can be used with your child, it must be reviewed and approved. We call that review
ERMA—the Enterprise Request Management Application. It's how we check that tools keep your
child's data safe and protected.

The review is also how we keep the promises New York State law makes to you. Under New York
State Education Law 2-d and its regulations, every vendor we work with must sign an agreement
about how it will handle student information and provide NYCPS an explanation of what data it
collects and how it protects it and meet recognized security standards. ERMA is where we confirm
all of that.

The ERMA process currently reviews tools for data privacy and security. NYCPS is committed to
building expanded evaluation capacity, including areas not covered by ERMA such as algorithmic
bias, equity impact, and instructional effectiveness.

Teachers and staff members can confirm whether a tool has been approved by visiting the NYCPS
Ed Tech Portal and logging in with their NYCPS credentials.

Families can also learn more about student privacy.

Student Privacy
• Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA): Gives families the right to access and
  protect their child's education records
• Children's Online Privacy Protection Rule (COPPA): Protects personal information collected from
  children under 13 online
• New York State Education Law Section 2-d: Protects student data and limits how it can be
  shared or used
• Chancellor's Regulation A-820: Data privacy and security
• Children's Internet Protection Act (CIPA): Requires schools to protect students from harmful
  online content
