    Washoe County AI Regulation 7212: Data and
            Student Privacy Passages
Excerpted verbatim from Administrative Regulation 7212 – Artificial Intelligence and Digital Learning (Washoe County
School District, Nevada; Version 1.1, revised November 7, 2025). Page numbers match the document's own page
numbering. Ellipses (…) mark omitted text. Outline labels (i., ii., a., etc.) are omitted.


Academics: Curriculum Philosophy (excerpt)
Source: page 3

Educators may:

• Utilize AI tools to support instructional planning, differentiation, communication, and accessibility
  for students with disabilities, provided the tool is approved through the District Digital Learning
  Tools process and so long as personally identifiable information (PII) or sensitive student data is
  not input into AI application, unless approved for such use

Operations: Operational Efficiency and Workflow Automation
(excerpt)
Source: page 5

Any AI implementation must be approved through the District Digital Learning Tools process and
evaluated for security and data privacy compliance prior to deployment and regularly monitored for
efficacy and reliability.

All District employees who choose to utilize AI applications are prohibited from inserting confidential
information, such as student personally identifiable information, into AI applications unless approved
for such use through District Digital Learning Tools process.

Operations: Staff Support and Professional Development (excerpt)
Source: page 6

AI tools supporting professional development (e.g., personalized learning platforms for staff training)
must be selected based on their capacity to provide actionable insights, promote skill growth, and
respect user privacy. AI must be used to enhance learning opportunities without infringing on
employee privacy or misrepresenting their capabilities.

Operations: Student Support Services and Intervention (excerpt)
Source: page 6

AI may act in the capacity of an early warning system, assisting in identifying trends in student
behavior, performance, and engagement across the district to support data-driven interventions.

However, any AI application in this context must operate under strict confidentiality agreements,
adhere to federal and state privacy laws (e.g., FERPA), and require oversight by human
professionals when determining specific interventions.

Operations: Vendor and Tool Evaluation
Source: pages 6–7

Prior to use, all AI applications must undergo a rigorous assessment for alignment with District
policies on data security, privacy, and ethical use through the District Digital Learning Tool process.

Approved vendors must demonstrate compliance with the District’s privacy and data handling
standards, including a commitment to not using District data to train external models unless
explicitly permitted.

If Generative AI tools have not been evaluated or have been rejected from use, they may not be
used and may be blocked from the District network to prevent unauthorized data transfer and
restrict access to materials of potential harm.

Regardless of AI integration, all software must be approved prior to use within the District.
