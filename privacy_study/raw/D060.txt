Our Approach to AI in FCPS: Leverage AI Tools Ethically and
Responsibly (excerpt)
Source: web page, section 'Our Approach to AI in FCPS'

AI should be used in ways that protect privacy, support learning, uphold academic integrity, and
avoid harm.

• Protect privacy, security, and data rights of people in our community.

Protect Data and Privacy
Source: web page, section 'Protect Data and Privacy'

Protecting students, staff, and confidential information is a shared responsibility.

Staff are expected to:
• Never enter personally identifiable, confidential, or sensitive information into unapproved AI tools.
• Use only approved AI tools when working with protected information.
• Follow all applicable FCPS policies, state and federal laws, and data privacy requirements.
• Report suspected privacy or security concerns through established FCPS procedures.
What This Looks Like In Practice
Appropriate Use
• Drafting a family newsletter without student information.
• Creating lesson ideas using general prompts.
• Brainstorming instructional strategies.
Not Appropriate
• Uploading student work containing names to an unapproved AI tool.
• Entering IEP, 504, disciplinary, or personnel information into public AI tools.

Check AI Tool Approval Before Use
Source: web page, section 'Check AI Tool Approval Before Use'

Not all AI tools have been reviewed for FCPS use.

Staff are expected to:
• Verify approval in the Digital Ecosystem Library (DEL).
• Follow the approved audience and usage guidance.
• Use only approved AI tools on FCPS devices and networks.

Partnering with Families: Key Messages and Making Informed
Choices About AI Tools
Source: web page, section 'Partnering with Families'

Protecting student data and privacy is a shared priority between schools and families.

Making Informed Choices About AI Tools (sample family letter)
Ask these quick questions together:

• What happens to the data?
• Does the tool collect, share, or store personal information?
• Who owns the work?
• Will your child keep rights to their writing, images, or projects once entered?
• Can data be deleted later?
• Is there an option to remove information if you change your mind?
• Is it age-appropriate?
• Many tools have age requirements (often 13+ or 18+).
Reviewing privacy policies and terms of service helps your child build responsible digital habits and
keeps their ideas at the center of learning.

Ideas for Family Conversations (excerpt)
Source: web page, section 'Partnering with Families'

• Most AI tools are designed for ages 13+ (some 18+). Families decide what’s appropriate at
  home.
• Teachers will only use AI tools approved in the DEL. No student will be required to use
  unapproved tools.

Guidance on Personal Devices: How Families Can Support
Source: web page, section 'Partnering with Families'

• Read the tool’s terms of service and privacy policy with your child.
• Discuss what kind of information the tool collects, how it may be used, and whether data can be
  deleted.
• Confirm age requirements for use.
• Talk about who owns the work once it’s entered and whether rights remain with the student.
• Encourage your child to pause and ask: “What information am I sharing, and do I feel
  comfortable with this?"
