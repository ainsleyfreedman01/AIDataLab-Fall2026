Here are **8 options**, designed for an audience of school administrators, students, and community members. **These are suggestions only—I won’t create or modify anything yet.**

## 1. Three-bar overview: “What student-data issues do the excerpts discuss?”
**Type:** Simple horizontal bar chart.

Three bars with numbers printed directly beside them:
- Sharing or using data for other purposes: **78 of 184**
- Using data to train or improve AI: **52 of 184**
- Keeping or deleting data: **37 of 184**

**Why it’s clearer:** No stacked sections or legend to decode. It answers one question immediately.

**Key caution:** Discussion does not equal protection. Topics overlap.

**Best use:** Your first results slide. **Highest priority.**

## 2. Dataset breakdown: “What did we actually study?”
**Type:** Branching count diagram.

**205 inventory documents**
→ **184 had privacy-excerpt PDFs analyzed**
→ **21 had no privacy passage found in the material reviewed**

**Why it’s clearer:** Explains where the denominator of 184 comes from before presenting percentages.

**Key caution:** The 21 are not necessarily documents without privacy protections; access and source-selection limitations affected some entries.

**Best use:** A short methods slide.

## 3. Simplified profile chart: “Instructions or questions to ask?”
**Type:** Two horizontal stacked bars with **three**, rather than four, groups.

Combine “direction only” and “direction and review” into **“Includes an instruction or recommendation.”**

| Topic | Includes an instruction/recommendation | Review/transparency only | Other/excluded |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Training/improvement | 19 | 20 | 13 |
| Keeping/deleting data | 16 | 17 | 4 |

**Why it’s clearer:** Removes the difficult distinction between “direction only” and “both” while preserving the underlying counts.

**Key caution:** Label these as the **two topics examined in the detailed follow-up**, not the whole study. An instruction need not be specific or enforceable.

**Best use:** Optional deeper-results slide—not the opening chart.

## 4. Selected-practices chart: “What details do the excerpts address?”
**Type:** Horizontal bars showing a small, clearly labeled selection of practices.

For example:
- Disclosure to others: **63**
- Limits on what data can be used for: **40**
- How long data is kept: **29**
- Deletion procedures: **22**
- Requests to delete data: **7**

All are counts out of **184 documents**.

**Why it’s clearer:** Uses recognizable questions rather than broad research terminology.

**Key caution:** These are discussion counts—including review questions—not counts of guaranteed rights or requirements. Mark the chart as **selected practices**, not an exhaustive list.

**Best use:** A second results slide.

## 5. Personal-information chart: “What kinds of information are named?”
**Type:** Short horizontal bar chart.

Show selected categories such as:
- Names
- Education records
- Health information
- Home addresses
- Grades/performance
- Disability/IEP information

**Why it’s clearer:** Makes “personal information” tangible and relevant to everyday school activities.

**Key caution:** This analysis uses **district-level counts**, not the 184-document denominator. Naming more categories does not automatically mean stronger protection.

**Best use:** Student- and community-focused slide. **High priority if PII is part of your presentation.**

## 6. Distribution chart: “How detailed are the personal-data lists?”
**Type:** Ordered bar chart grouping districts by how many personal-data categories their excerpts name.

Possible groups:
- No specific categories named
- 1–3 categories
- 4–7 categories
- 8 or more categories

**Why it’s clearer:** Summarizes the dense district-by-PII matrix without displaying dozens of district names.

**Key caution:** These group totals would need to be calculated and checked. “No specific categories named” does not mean “no privacy language” or “no protection.”

**Best use:** Optional companion to suggestion 5.

## 7. Side-by-side evidence cards: “What does the difference look like in actual language?”
**Type:** Qualitative comparison using short, verified source quotations.

One card shows **reviewing a vendor’s practices**; another shows **an instruction or recommendation**. Highlight the relevant words and include the source and location.

**Why it’s clearer:** Shows what the coding means instead of asking the audience to trust abstract labels.

**Key caution:** Verify quotations against original sources and identify guidance, templates, or drafts appropriately. Selected examples illustrate differences; they do not establish how common each difference is.

**Best use:** Immediately before a profile chart—or instead of it. **Highest priority.**

## 8. Robustness check: “Does the overall pattern change when we change the selection?”
**Type:** Paired-dot chart.

For each topic, compare coverage in:
- All 184 excerpts
- The 132 longer excerpts with at least 1,000 body characters

**Why it’s helpful:** Shows that longer excerpts have higher coverage, while sharing remains most frequently discussed and retention/deletion least frequently discussed.

**Key caution:** The longer-excerpt group is a subset, not an independent comparison group. This demonstrates sensitivity to excerpt selection—not proof that the findings are unbiased.

**Best use:** College-level methods discussion or appendix.
